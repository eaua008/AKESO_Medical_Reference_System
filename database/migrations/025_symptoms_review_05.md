## Involuntary jerky movements (chorea)  (`sym_chorea`)

**Added to the description:** The movements mainly affect the face and the hands and feet and are often blended into semi-purposeful actions to disguise them. They arise from reduced inhibition in the basal ganglia; Huntington disease is the commonest degenerative cause, and Sydenham chorea is usually self-limited.

**New causes:**
- Phenytoin, cocaine, tricyclic antidepressants or oral contraceptives
- Tardive dyskinesia after long-term antipsychotics

**New red flags:**
- Family history of a similar movement disorder or early dementia (possible Huntington disease)

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Chorea, Athetosis, and Hemiballismus: Rajput A, Noyes E. Chorea, Athetosis, and Hemiballismus. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Mar 2026. https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/neurologic-disorders/movement-and-cerebellar-disorders/chorea-athetosis-and-hemiballismus

## Itching (pruritus)  (`sym_pruritus`)

**Added to the description:** It is most often due to skin disease such as dry skin, dermatitis or fungal infection. Prominent itching without any visible skin changes should raise suspicion of a systemic, drug or nerve-related cause.

**New causes:**
- Dry skin (xerosis)
- Fungal skin infection
- Opioids and IV contrast
- Blood disorders such as lymphoma and polycythemia

**New red flags:**
- Itching with heavy urination and thirst
- Itching with numbness, tingling or weakness in the limbs

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Itching: Benedetti J. Itching. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Jan 2024 (updated Apr 2025). https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/dermatologic-disorders/approach-to-the-dermatologic-patient/itching

## Jaundice  (`sym_jaundice`)

**Added to the description:** It can be visible from a bilirubin of about 2 to 3 mg/dL (34 to 51 µmol/L). Very high aminotransferases (over 500 U/L) with alkaline phosphatase under three times normal suggest liver-cell injury, whereas lower aminotransferases with alkaline phosphatase over three times normal suggest cholestasis.

**New causes:**
- Autoimmune hepatitis
- Cancer of the pancreas or bile ducts

**New red flags:**
- Vomiting blood or black stools

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Jaundice: Tholey D. Jaundice. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Aug 2025. https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/approach-to-the-patient-with-liver-disease/jaundice

## Joint pain (arthralgia)  (`sym_arthralgia`)

**Added to the description:** Sudden pain in many joints is usually from a viral or other infection, a flare of inflammatory disease or crystal arthritis; chronic pain in many joints in adults is usually rheumatoid arthritis, spondyloarthritis or osteoarthritis. Pain at rest and on starting to move suggests inflammation, while pain that worsens with use and eases with rest suggests a mechanical cause.

**New causes:**
- Reactive arthritis after infection
- Systemic lupus erythematosus
- Spondyloarthritis

**New red flags:**
- Joint pain with fever, rash, red eyes or weight loss

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Pain in Multiple Joints: Villa-Forte A. Pain in Multiple Joints. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Apr 2025. https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/musculoskeletal-and-connective-tissue-disorders/pain-in-and-around-joints/pain-in-multiple-joints

## Joint swelling  (`sym_joint_swelling`)

**Added to the description:** Septic arthritis is the most dangerous cause at any age, so a new swollen joint with fluid needs joint aspiration (cell count, Gram stain, culture and crystal search). Finding crystals does not rule out infection, and a joint white cell count over 50,000/µL can occur in both.

**New causes:**
- Bleeding into the joint (hemarthrosis) on anticoagulants or with a bleeding disorder
- Lyme disease

**New red flags:**
- Joint swelling in someone on anticoagulants or with a bleeding disorder

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Pain in and Around a Single Joint: Villa-Forte A. Pain in and Around a Single Joint. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Apr 2025 (updated Jun 2025). https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/musculoskeletal-and-connective-tissue-disorders/pain-in-and-around-joints/pain-in-and-around-a-single-joint

## Koplik spots  (`sym_koplik_spots`)

**Added to the description:** They are seen in about 70% of cases, usually opposite the first and second upper molars, and may persist for a day or two after the rash appears. They follow a prodrome of fever, cough, runny nose and conjunctivitis; the rash then spreads from the head down, and patients are contagious from 4 days before to 4 days after rash onset.

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Measles: de St. Maurice A. Measles. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Jun 2025 (updated Sep 2025). https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/common-viral-infections-in-infants-and-children/measles
- CDC - Clinical Overview of Measles: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Clinical Overview of Measles. Atlanta (GA): CDC; reviewed May 2025 (updated Aug 2026). https://www.cdc.gov/measles/hcp/clinical-overview/index.html

## Leg swelling (edema)  (`sym_edema`)

**Added to the description:** Heart failure gives symmetrical, painless pitting edema in the lowest parts of the body, whereas a sudden, painful swelling of one leg suggests deep vein thrombosis and usually needs an ultrasound. Generalised edema mostly comes from heart, liver or kidney disease.

**New causes:**
- Lymphatic obstruction (lymphedema)
- Cellulitis
- Angioedema
- Low blood protein from malnutrition

**New red flags:**
- Sudden onset of swelling
- Swelling of the face, lips or tongue

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Edema: Thompson AD, Shea MJ. Edema. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Aug 2024 (updated Oct 2025). https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/cardiovascular-disorders/symptoms-of-cardiovascular-disorders/edema

## Loss of appetite  (`sym_anorexia`)

**Added to the description:** Weight loss with a poor appetite most often comes from depression, cancer, medicine side effects or substance use, whereas weight loss despite a good appetite suggests hyperthyroidism, uncontrolled diabetes or malabsorption.

**New causes:**
- Substance use
- Malaria
- HIV infection

**New red flags:**
- Swollen lymph nodes, bone pain or coughing blood
- Fear of weight gain in a young person (possible eating disorder)

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Involuntary Weight Loss: Wasserman MR. Involuntary Weight Loss. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Feb 2025 (updated Jan 2026). https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/special-subjects/nonspecific-symptoms/involuntary-weight-loss

## Loss of smell or taste  (`sym_anosmia`)

**Added to the description:** Most people with anosmia can still taste salty, sweet, sour and bitter but report a loss of taste because flavour depends on smell. Head injury is the main cause in young adults and Alzheimer disease in older adults, and smell loss persists after an upper respiratory infection in a minority of people.

**New causes:**
- Alzheimer disease (older adults)
- Medicines and toxins
- Radiation or nasal surgery
- Influenza

**New red flags:**
- Sudden loss of smell

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Anosmia: Fried MP. Anosmia. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Mar 2025 (updated Sep 2026). https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/ear-nose-and-throat-disorders/approach-to-the-patient-with-nasal-and-pharyngeal-symptoms/anosmia

**New disease links:**

## Mucosal bleeding / petechiae (gum bleeding, nosebleed)  (`sym_bleeding`)

**Added to the description:** Bleeding into the skin and mucous membranes with petechiae suggests a platelet or blood-vessel problem, while deep bleeding into joints and muscles suggests a clotting-factor disorder. The first tests are a full blood count, blood smear, PT and aPTT.

**New causes:**
- Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) in sepsis
- Kidney failure (uremic platelet dysfunction)
- Von Willebrand disease
- NSAIDs and aspirin

**New red flags:**
- Bleeding in pregnancy
- Bloody diarrhea with low platelets (possible hemolytic uremic syndrome)

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Excessive Bleeding: Streiff MB. Excessive Bleeding. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Sep 2025. https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/hematology-and-oncology/hemostasis/excessive-bleeding

**New disease links:**
- leptospirosis (3/10, x0.5)
- chronic_kidney_disease (2/10, x0.3)

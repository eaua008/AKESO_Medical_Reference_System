## Muscle aches (myalgia)  (`sym_myalgia`)

**Added to the description:** In adults over 50, aching and morning stiffness lasting over an hour in both shoulders and hips, with a markedly raised ESR and CRP but no true weakness, suggests polymyalgia rheumatica; 40 to 60% of patients with giant cell arteritis also have it.

**New causes:**
- Polymyalgia rheumatica (adults over 50)
- Inflammatory myositis

**New red flags:**
- Shoulder and hip stiffness with headache, jaw pain or vision changes in someone over 50

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Polymyalgia Rheumatica: Villa-Forte A. Polymyalgia Rheumatica. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Dec 2024. https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/musculoskeletal-and-connective-tissue-disorders/vasculitis/polymyalgia-rheumatica

## Muscle cramps  (`sym_muscle_cramps`)

**Added to the description:** Most cramps are benign night-time calf cramps or cramps during exercise, helped by tight calf muscles, dehydration, low potassium or magnesium and some medicines. Stretching and fluids are preferred; drugs for cramps have little proven benefit.

**New causes:**
- Benign night-time leg cramps
- Low magnesium

**New red flags:**
- Cramps in the arms or trunk, or with muscle twitching, wasting or weakness

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Muscle Cramps: Freedman M. Muscle Cramps. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Aug 2025. https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/neurologic-disorders/symptoms-of-neurologic-disorders/muscle-cramps

## Nausea & vomiting  (`sym_nausea_vomiting`)

**Added to the description:** The commonest causes are gastroenteritis, medicines or drugs and toxins. Repeated cycles of vomiting in an adult who uses cannabis heavily, eased by hot showers, suggest cannabinoid hyperemesis, which settles when cannabis is stopped; a pregnancy test is advised in women who could be pregnant.

**New causes:**
- Cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome
- Toxins and poisoning
- Bowel obstruction
- Diabetic ketoacidosis

**New red flags:**
- Swollen, drum-like abdomen (possible bowel obstruction)
- Vomiting with confusion or drowsiness

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Nausea and Vomiting: Gotfried J. Nausea and Vomiting. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Apr 2026 (updated Jun 2026). https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/gastrointestinal-disorders/symptoms-of-gastrointestinal-disorders/nausea-and-vomiting

## Night sweats  (`sym_night_sweats`)

**Added to the description:** In active TB they come with fever, weight loss, poor appetite and fatigue, plus a cough of 3 weeks or more in pulmonary TB. In lymphoma, fever, drenching night sweats and weight loss of more than 10% in 6 months are the 'B symptoms' that affect staging and prognosis.

**New causes:**
- HIV infection

**New red flags:**
- Weight loss of more than 10% in 6 months
- Cough lasting 3 weeks or more

**New references:**
- CDC - Signs and Symptoms of Tuberculosis: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Signs and Symptoms of Tuberculosis. Atlanta (GA): CDC; reviewed Mar 2024. https://www.cdc.gov/tb/signs-symptoms/index.html
- Merck Manual Professional - Hodgkin Lymphoma: Martin P, Leonard JP. Hodgkin Lymphoma. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Mar 2024 (updated Apr 2026). https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/oncology/lymphomas/hodgkin-lymphoma

## One-sided weakness or numbness  (`sym_hemiparesis`)

**Added to the description:** Arm and leg weakness on one side with spasticity, brisk reflexes and an upgoing plantar response points to a lesion in the opposite brain hemisphere. Weakness that becomes severe within minutes is usually stroke or injury.

**New causes:**
- Weakness after a seizure (Todd paralysis)

**New red flags:**
- Weakness reaching its worst within minutes

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Weakness: Freedman M. Weakness. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Aug 2025. https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/neurologic-disorders/symptoms-of-neurologic-disorders/weakness

## Pain or tingling at an animal-bite site  (`sym_bite_site_paresthesia`)

**Added to the description:** The incubation period averages 1 to 2 months but can exceed a year, and brain symptoms usually follow within about 2 weeks of the first symptoms. Immediate wound washing and post-exposure prophylaxis (vaccine plus rabies immune globulin) after a bite prevent the disease; any contact with a bat is an indication.

**New red flags:**
- Any contact with a bat

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Rabies: Klein RS. Rabies. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Jul 2026. https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/neurologic-disorders/brain-infections/rabies
- CDC - Clinical Features of Rabies: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Clinical Features of Rabies. Atlanta (GA): CDC, National Center for Emerging and Zoonotic Infectious Diseases; reviewed Jul 2025. https://www.cdc.gov/rabies/hcp/clinical-signs/index.html

## Painful mouth sores or ulcers  (`sym_mouth_sores`)

**Added to the description:** The commonest causes are recurrent aphthous ulcers, herpes viruses, Candida, injury and irritants, and chemotherapy or radiation. Mouth sores alone without fever or other signs are usually viral or aphthous; blistering skin that peels with light rubbing (Nikolsky sign) suggests Stevens-Johnson syndrome or pemphigus.

**New causes:**
- Oral thrush (Candida)
- Chemotherapy or radiation
- Stevens-Johnson syndrome or pemphigus

**New red flags:**
- Fever with skin blisters or red, painful eyes

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Stomatitis: Hennessy BJ. Stomatitis. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Mar 2026. https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/dental-disorders/symptoms-of-dental-and-oral-disorders/stomatitis

**New disease links:**

- hiv_infection (2/10, x0.3)

## Painful urination (dysuria)  (`sym_dysuria`)

**Added to the description:** Cystitis and sexually transmitted urethritis are the commonest causes. Pus from the urethra suggests gonorrhea, a tender prostate suggests prostatitis, and red cells without white cells with a sterile culture may point to a stone or cancer.

**New causes:**
- Bladder cancer (blood without infection)

**New red flags:**
- Recent catheter or urinary procedure
- Weakened immune system or known urinary tract abnormality

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Dysuria: Maddukuri G. Dysuria. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Sep 2024. https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/genitourinary-disorders/symptoms-of-genitourinary-disorders/dysuria

## Pale or clay-colored stools  (`sym_pale_stool`)

**Added to the description:** Cholestasis shows alkaline phosphatase and GGT more than three times normal with aminotransferases usually under 200 U/L, and dark urine may appear before jaundice is visible. Ultrasound is the first imaging test to look for a blocked bile duct.

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Jaundice: Tholey D. Jaundice. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Aug 2025. https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/approach-to-the-patient-with-liver-disease/jaundice

**New disease links:**

## Pale skin, lips or inner eyelids (pallor)  (`sym_pallor`)

**Added to the description:** Pallor is usually obvious only with severe anemia (hemoglobin under about 7 g/dL), so its absence does not rule anemia out. A reticulocyte count separates low production from blood loss or hemolysis.

**New causes:**
- Hemolysis (e.g., malaria)
- Intestinal worms and schistosomiasis

**New red flags:**
- Pallor with jaundice and dark urine (possible hemolysis)

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Evaluation of Anemia: Gerber GF. Evaluation of Anemia. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed May 2024. https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/hematology-and-oncology/approach-to-the-patient-with-anemia/evaluation-of-anemia

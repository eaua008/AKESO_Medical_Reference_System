## Excessive urination (polyuria)  (`sym_polyuria`)

**Added to the description:** Some references use more than 3 L a day. Uncontrolled diabetes and diuretics are the commonest causes, followed by primary polydipsia and diabetes insipidus; a high blood sodium suggests diabetes insipidus, while a low sodium suggests drinking too much water.

**New causes:**
- Primary polydipsia
- Lithium and other drugs that block vasopressin

**New red flags:**
- Onset in early childhood or a sudden, abrupt start
- Night sweats, cough or weight loss

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Polyuria: Maddukuri G. Polyuria. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Sep 2024. https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/genitourinary-disorders/symptoms-of-genitourinary-disorders/polyuria

## Facial droop  (`sym_facial_droop`)

**Added to the description:** Peripheral facial palsy has many causes besides Bell's palsy (usually herpes simplex): herpes zoster of the ear, Lyme disease, sarcoidosis, diabetes, skull fractures and tumors near the ear. MRI is needed when there are other neurological signs or the weakness comes on gradually.

**New causes:**
- Herpes zoster of the ear (Ramsay Hunt syndrome)
- Diabetes
- Sarcoidosis
- Skull base fracture

**New red flags:**
- Weakness on both sides of the face
- Weakness that develops slowly over weeks (possible tumor)

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Facial Nerve Palsy: Rubin M. Facial Nerve Palsy. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Aug 2025. https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/neurologic-disorders/neuro-ophthalmologic-and-cranial-nerve-disorders/facial-nerve-palsy

## Fatigue & malaise  (`sym_fatigue`)

**Added to the description:** The likely causes change with duration: under a month, mostly medicines, anemia, stress or depression; one to six months, diabetes, hypothyroidism, sleep apnea or cancer; beyond six months, chronic fatigue syndrome, post-viral or psychiatric causes. Fatigue that worsens with activity and improves with rest suggests a physical cause.

**New causes:**
- Medicines
- Obstructive sleep apnea
- Cancer
- Chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS) and post-viral fatigue

**New red flags:**
- Swollen lymph nodes in several areas, or fever with night sweats
- New headache or vision problems in an older adult (possible giant cell arteritis)

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Fatigue: Wasserman MR. Fatigue. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Feb 2025 (updated Jan 2026). https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/special-subjects/nonspecific-symptoms/fatigue

## Fear of water / throat spasms on swallowing (hydrophobia)  (`sym_hydrophobia`)

**Added to the description:** About 80% of rabies cases are furious, with agitation, hallucinations and insomnia; the remaining 20% are paralytic, with ascending paralysis and no hydrophobia. The incubation period averages 1 to 2 months but can exceed a year, death usually follows within days of symptom onset, and prompt post-exposure prophylaxis almost always prevents the disease.

**New red flags:**
- Pain, itching or tingling at the site of an old bite

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Rabies: Klein RS. Rabies. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Jul 2026 (updated Sep 2026). https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/neurologic-disorders/brain-infections/rabies
- CDC - Clinical Features of Rabies: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Clinical Features of Rabies. Atlanta (GA): CDC; reviewed Jul 2025 (updated Mar 2026). https://www.cdc.gov/rabies/hcp/clinical-signs/index.html

## Fever  (`sym_fever`)

**Added to the description:** An oral temperature of 37.8 °C or more after early morning, or 37.2 °C or more in the early morning, is raised; fever differs from hyperthermia (such as heat stroke), where the set point is normal. Older adults may have little or no fever despite a serious infection.

**New causes:**
- Abscess or other hidden infection
- Heat stroke (hyperthermia, not a true fever)

**New red flags:**
- Temperature above 40 °C or below 35 °C
- Recent travel or a weakened immune system

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Fever: Bush LM, Vazquez-Pertejo MT. Fever. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Jul 2024 (updated Apr 2026). https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/infectious-diseases/biology-of-infectious-disease/fever

## Flank pain  (`sym_flank_pain`)

**Added to the description:** Kidney stone colic is excruciating, comes in waves of 20 to 60 minutes with nausea, and makes the patient writhe or pace, unlike peritonitis where the patient lies still. Pain spreading across the abdomen suggests a stone high in the ureter, and pain spreading to the groin or genitals suggests one lower down.

**New causes:**
- Aortic dissection or aneurysm (older patients)

**New red flags:**
- Fever or cloudy urine with a blocked kidney (needs urgent drainage)

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Urinary Calculi: Preminger GM. Urinary Calculi. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Jan 2025 (updated Apr 2025). https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/genitourinary-disorders/urinary-calculi/urinary-calculi

## Foamy or bubbly urine  (`sym_foamy_urine`)

**Added to the description:** Glomerular disease is the commonest cause of heavy proteinuria, including diabetic nephropathy, membranous nephropathy and focal segmental glomerulosclerosis; nephrotic-range proteinuria is more than 3.5 g a day. Persistent proteinuria on a dipstick should be measured (urine protein-to-creatinine ratio) and investigated.

**New causes:**
- Membranous nephropathy and focal segmental glomerulosclerosis

**New red flags:**
- Persistent foamy urine in someone with diabetes or high blood pressure

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Proteinuria: Maddukuri G. Proteinuria. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Sep 2024 (updated Feb 2026). https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/genitourinary-disorders/symptoms-of-genitourinary-disorders/proteinuria

**New disease links:**

## Headache  (`sym_headache`)

**Added to the description:** A new headache after age 50 is treated as secondary until proven otherwise. A sudden thunderclap headache needs urgent brain imaging and, if imaging is normal, a lumbar puncture to look for bleeding.

**New causes:**
- Cluster headache
- Giant cell arteritis (older adults)
- Medication-overuse headache
- Carbon monoxide poisoning

**New red flags:**
- Jaw pain on chewing, scalp tenderness or vision changes in someone over 50
- Headache in a person with cancer or a weakened immune system

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Approach to the Patient With Headache: Silberstein SD. Approach to the Patient With Headache. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Feb 2025. https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/neurologic-disorders/headache/approach-to-the-patient-with-headache

## Heartburn  (`sym_heartburn`)

**Added to the description:** Heartburn, with or without regurgitation, is the main symptom of GERD in adolescents and adults. Long-standing reflux can lead to esophagitis, stricture, Barrett esophagus and adenocarcinoma, and reflux into the airway can cause chronic cough, hoarseness or wheeze.

**New causes:**
- Eosinophilic esophagitis

**New red flags:**
- No improvement after a course of acid-suppressing medicine
- Hoarseness, chronic cough or wheeze

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD): Lynch KL. Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD). Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Feb 2026. https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/gastrointestinal-disorders/esophageal-and-swallowing-disorders/gastroesophageal-reflux-disease-gerd

## Heat intolerance and excessive sweating  (`sym_heat_intolerance`)

**Added to the description:** It reflects increased sensitivity to adrenaline and comes with warm moist skin, tremor and a fast heart rate. Eye changes (bulging eyes) and shin skin thickening point specifically to Graves disease; older adults may have apathetic hyperthyroidism with low mood, weight loss and atrial fibrillation rather than heat intolerance.

**New causes:**
- Taking too much thyroid hormone medicine

**New red flags:**
- Irregular heartbeat (possible atrial fibrillation)

**New references:**
- Merck Manual Professional - Hyperthyroidism: Boucai L. Hyperthyroidism. Merck Manual Professional Version. Rahway (NJ): Merck & Co.; reviewed Jan 2026. https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/endocrine-and-metabolic-disorders/thyroid-disorders/hyperthyroidism
